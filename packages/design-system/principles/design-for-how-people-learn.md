# Design for How People Learn in this product

Julie Dirksen writes about teaching, and most of Weave is not a course. But every screen that gives steps to follow, a list to choose from or a card to judge asks a person to take something in and use it. That is where this book applies.

The copy the team holds is chapter 4, "How do we remember?", and these notes cover that chapter only. Page numbers are the book's own.

## Attention

- **People stop seeing what never changes** (86-88). A sign in the same place on every page, saying the same thing, is tuned out the way banners are. Say a status where it answers a question and nowhere else: the header says how a card is saved only on the pages where the card is worked on (DL-059).
- **Sameness frees attention for the content, and needless difference irritates** (87). The same control looks and works the same on every list (DL-061). Vary only where the difference means something.
- **A thing is noticed when it matters to the person, is being looked for, or calls for action** (88). Lead a card with what the person came for: how strong a fit is, and why (DL-064).

## Working memory

- **It is small and it empties fast** (88-91). Never ask a person to carry a fact from one screen to the next. Show it again where it is used.
- **Group long lists** (91-93). A long flat list overwhelms, and the same items under a few headings can be taken in. Twelve ways to apply became three (DL-062). Steps come in twos and threes, and a long menu reads in columns.
- **A fact in an unfamiliar form says nothing about what to do** (89). A temperature in the wrong scale does not tell you whether to take a coat. Say what a thing means for the person, such as "They offer what you need", and never show a bare label or a score.

## Finding it again

- **More ways in make a thing easier to find again** (94-96). An idea reached through a word, an icon, a colour and a place holds better than one reached through a label alone. A kind of support has a name, a glyph and a hue for this reason.
- **Learn it where you will use it** (97-99). Help belongs at the point of need, on the device and the screen in question. The install guide shows the steps for the phone in hand, with the signs that phone shows (DL-067).
- **Feeling is part of the setting** (100-102). People meet errors and closing dates under pressure. Write those messages for someone hurried.

## Recognising and recalling

- **Recognising is easier than recalling** (103-104). Offer choices to pick from before a blank to fill, and show the sign to look for instead of naming it.
- **A job aid turns "remember the steps" into "follow the steps"** (107). Numbered steps beside the task do this. Give them at the moment of the task, not on a help page.
- **Familiar is not the same as known** (107-108). People nod at a list they could not act on. In testing, judge understanding by what a person does next.

## Stories and habits

- **Stories are remembered and lists of facts are not** (112-113). A story has an order, people and a point. An initiative's own words say more than its tags, so a card quotes them.
- **Practised steps stop needing attention** (115-117). Keep what people do often in the same place and the same order, so it becomes habit.

## Saying it again

- **Repeating helps, but the same thing said the same way is tuned out** (119-122). If something matters, say it again in another place and form, where it is used.

## Where the book stops

This is one chapter of a book about teaching adults, read for the parts of Weave that instruct, list or ask for a judgement. It says nothing about navigation, layout or visual hierarchy; the other books do. Like them, it is guidance. When watching real people shows otherwise, believe the people (see the [testing plan](usability-testing.md)).
