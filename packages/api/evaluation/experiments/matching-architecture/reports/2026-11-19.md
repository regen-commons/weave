# Matching architecture experiment: results, 19 November 2026

Run on Node 24.20.0, Apple M4, from commit `3b8df8b` plus this experiment. Full
output is in the three JSON files beside this one. The
[experiment README](../README.md) has the commands.

## What this does and does not show

- Retrieval was scored on the v3 holdout, which had been examined before this work.
  Two of the variants below were added after the first scored run. Treat every
  number as directional and collect a new holdout before making a quality claim.
- The holdout is small and synthetic: 64 candidates, 45 agreed queries with a
  relevant candidate and 8 with none, 9 of the 45 in Portuguese. One query moves a
  language group by about 11 points.
- The team examples use fictional initiatives written by the same agent as the
  code. They prove the rules hold, not that matches are good.

## 2. Retrieval

`retrieval-eval.mjs` on the v3 holdout, all candidates, post-hoc scoring
definitions from the [evaluation README](../../../README.md). This harness
reproduces the committed result for the current matcher exactly.

Thresholds come from the v2 development split only (88 queries, 100 passages), by
a rule written before scoring: the lowest threshold at which semantic-only
suggestions there are at least 80% relevant and no no-match query gets one. The
relative threshold and the veto were added after the first scored run; their
cut-offs come from development by the same kind of rule.

Brackets are 95% bootstrap intervals over the 45 positive queries.

| Engine | Relevant among top-five suggestions | Recall at five | Positive queries with no suggestion | No-match queries with a suggestion |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Current matcher (`partner-concepts-v2`) | 24 of 36, 67% [48 to 89] | 44% [30 to 58] | 21 of 45 | 2 of 8 |
| Pinned English model, semantic only, cosine 0.45 | 32 of 78, 41% | 58% | 11 | 3 |
| Pinned English model, hybrid with veto | 34 of 41, 83% [71 to 95] | 64% [50 to 77] | 12 | 5 |
| multilingual-e5-small, semantic only, cosine 0.82 | 41 of 139, 30% | 75% | 3 | 6 |
| multilingual-e5-small, hybrid with veto | 39 of 58, 67% [57 to 78] | 73% [61 to 84] | 3 | 6 |
| multilingual MiniLM-L12, semantic only, cosine 0.57 | 31 of 32, 97% [90 to 100] | 57% [43 to 70] | 17 | 0 |
| multilingual MiniLM-L12, semantic only, relative threshold | 34 of 35, 97% [91 to 100] | 66% [54 to 78] | 10 | 2 |
| multilingual MiniLM-L12, hybrid (keyword or semantic) | 40 of 53, 75% [61 to 91] | 73% [60 to 84] | 8 | 2 |
| multilingual MiniLM-L12, hybrid with veto | 39 of 43, 91% [83 to 98] | 73% [60 to 84] | 9 | 2 |

On the 19 queries where the two judges disagreed or doubted, which are never
scored, the same ordering holds: current 65% relevant and 46% recall, MiniLM-L12
hybrid with veto 86% and 60%.

Size and time, measured in Node on this machine with quantized weights on CPU, one
short text at a time, model files already on disk. These are not browser or phone
measurements.

| Model | Download (weights, tokenizer, configuration) | Load | Embed one short text |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Pinned English model | 25.4 MB | 0.8 s | 4 ms |
| multilingual-e5-small | 135.4 MB | 2.1 s | 10 ms |
| multilingual MiniLM-L12 | 135.4 MB | 1.3 s | 9 ms |

What these runs say:

1. A threshold chosen on one fixture does not carry to another for two of the three
   models. Cosine scales differ by model: multilingual-e5-small scores almost every
   pair above 0.8, so a small shift in the cut-off changes everything.
2. The pinned English model cannot serve Portuguese. Its semantic path returns a
   suggestion for all three Portuguese no-match queries.
3. Adding keyword evidence to the multilingual MiniLM model raises recall from 57%
   to 73%, and almost every wrong suggestion in that hybrid comes from the keyword
   rule. Letting the model veto keyword evidence removes 12 of the 15.
4. Fusing keyword and semantic ranks made ranking worse than the semantic rank
   alone on this corpus.

## 3. Gap-driven teams

`teams-demo.mjs`, ten forward and three reverse examples on real catalog programs
and fictional initiatives. All thirteen end as expected and the twelve checks in
`prototype.test.mjs` pass.

| Example | Result |
|---|---|
| Community group and a US climate adaptation grant that asks for three kinds of organization | Two shared initiatives cover both open asks. The practitioner would be the applicant, and the result says so |
| The same, seen by someone who has shared nothing | "2 partners, 2 of 2 covered", no names or reasons |
| The same, with no shared local government | One of two asks covered. A public project that describes the same role is listed as a lead to check and is not counted |
| Kenyan network and a program with a sourced Dutch-partner condition, opened from the fund's page | One shared Dutch foundation covers the ask |
| The same program, not requested | Nothing: the program's documented purpose is too broad to establish a fit |
| Kenyan work and a program that funds work only in the United States | Nothing: a partner cannot change where the work happens |
| Applicant must be based in Fiji | Nothing: no shared initiative based there offers to lead |
| A scholarship for individuals | Nothing |
| Partnership of at least three organizations, one shared candidate | One of two partner places covered |
| A charity with no sourced gap | No team needed |
| Reverse: two initiatives with a confirmed fit | One program, because the partner closes one of two asks |
| Reverse: two initiatives with no confirmed fit | Nothing |

Two findings changed the design:

- Keyword matching alone picked a wrong partner in two of four compared examples:
  an Italian cooperative as the "local government partner", and a US county office
  as a "third-sector organization". A condition on the ask, checked against a fact
  the partner shared, removed both.
- The "theme" reason in the reverse direction (the program's purpose is covered by
  the two descriptions together and by neither alone) fired for no program in
  either pair. It stays unproven and is not part of the recommendation.
