# Weave with reusable project identity: business model canvas

Status: research draft for Regen Commons team and council review · 6 November 2026. All new policies, commercial ideas and targets are proposals.

Companions: [Partner brief](partner-brief.md) · [Technical spec](technical-spec.md) · [Design spec](design-spec.md).

## Starting point

This updates the **existing**, not reconstructed, [31 October 2026 business model canvas](../../02-business-model-canvas.md), read on upstream [`495e95a`](https://github.com/regen-commons/funding-commons/tree/495e95ae6ea27521f355bc7f10af1488a1e290d9). It preserves its commons mission, nine blocks and attention to governance and value returned. The core purpose remains helping regenerative initiatives coordinate the support they need and can offer, across places and eight forms of capital. Certified/Hypercerts profile reuse would reduce repeated entry and make Weave a useful application within a shared ecosystem.

The original canvas includes dated catalog counts, named grant windows, infrastructure prices and 2027 revenue estimates. Those are historical planning assumptions, not a current forecast or verified funding pipeline. This draft does not repeat them as available funding or quotes. Recheck specific programs and costs before a budget or application decision. No revenue, partner contribution, membership growth or pilot recruitment is committed.

The product baseline is the [PRD](../../04-prd.md), [proposal and Phase 0 deadline](../../01-proposal-brief.md), and [live private-saving scope](https://github.com/regen-commons/funding-commons/blob/495e95ae6ea27521f355bc7f10af1488a1e290d9/docs/platform/08-accounts-and-saving.md#private-account-connection). Reusing a public project is optional; Certified is the only sign-in for private saving, with no separate Weave registration or migration. Public browsing, unlinked work and the distinction between an account and membership remain central recommendations.

## 1. Customer and participant segments

| Segment | Job Weave can serve | Evidence or assumption |
|---|---|---|
| Place-based project teams and informal initiatives | Find timely support and useful partners without repeatedly writing a profile | Core segment in the existing PRD/canvas. Existing Certified projects are a proposed initial reuse cohort, not the whole market. |
| Researchers, tool builders, artists and culture bearers | Offer skills, methods and resources; find complementary work | Established Weave lenses. Do not require land ownership, incorporation or a Certified project to participate. |
| Collaborators and network stewards | Keep shared needs/offers current and coordinate introductions | Established need; multi-editor Weave service remains future work. |
| Regen Commons members, including people without projects | Use a practical commons utility, learn, and contribute to others' work | Membership utility is an existing objective. Entitlements and organizational membership treatment remain council policy. |
| Funding partners and allocators | Understand regional needs and potential partnerships, with traceable information | Existing canvas segment. Demand for a paid service is unvalidated; catalog inclusion does not imply a relationship. |
| Protocol and application partners | See reusable records used in a second workflow and learn where interoperability fails | New proposed partner segment, supported by public ecosystem reuse. Partners are not assumed to be paying customers. |

Ma Earth's funding rounds demonstrate an existing population of project records, not a guaranteed Weave audience. Hypercerts reported 201 funded projects in its 18 September case study; recruitment willingness and cross-app adoption still need testing. [Official case study](https://hypercerts.org/blog/3mvru46xfus2r).

## 2. Value propositions

**For project teams:** reuse checked public context, then focus on today's needs and offers. Keep private work private, with a useful typed path on a phone. Find funding with reasons and uncertainty, and later discover complementary partners through separately consented sharing. The benefit is less repetition and better coordination, not an endorsement or funding guarantee.

**For members and stewards:** make varied contributions legible, including social, cultural, living and spiritual support that a money-only application misses. Keep current information through useful return visits rather than requesting updates only for a funding round. This continues the [original canvas](../../02-business-model-canvas.md) and [PRD goals](../../04-prd.md).

**For Ma Earth:** hypothesized repeat engagement between rounds, corrections to stale source details and links back to original project records. **For Hypercerts/Certified:** a real consumer of project identity, field mapping and organizational permissions, with a possible future needs/offers vocabulary. **For funding partners:** consented, current context about potential collaborations. These benefits require evidence; external presence must not be used as a proxy for project quality.

The shared-profile hypothesis has technical support: public profile and project reads succeeded. It does not remove Weave's private workflow responsibility. [Research findings](technical-spec.md#1-what-exists-today).

## 3. Channels

Retain the website, existing network calls, practical offers-and-needs sessions, and curated funding work from the original plan. Add an optional “Use an existing project” entry in Weave and, only by partner agreement, links from Certified or Ma Earth into a specific project workflow.

Start with facilitated sessions that solve a current task. A cohort should leave with checked asks and a saved private copy, not merely a new account. Return visits can follow a useful project update or chosen funding reminder when those services exist. Weekly email remains opt-in future service work. Do not assume partner mailing lists, endorsement badges or promotional placement are available.

## 4. Participant relationships

Use self-service with visible value before sign-in, supplemented by light steward help. Teams control whether they use an external profile, save privately, invite colleagues, share within Weave or publish to an open network. Explain each choice where it matters.

Offer a reliable path back: resume the draft, see current asks, confirm or retire them, and check changed source details. Team collaboration should outlast one person's involvement through more than one maintainer and explicit handover. Keep support for groups without an upstream identity and people whose role is collaborator, not owner. [Design proposal](design-spec.md), [team and recovery specification](technical-spec.md#6-teams-authorization-and-continuity).

Trust depends on matching promises to working services. Do not claim an invitation, reminder, correction or introduction was delivered when the adapter is only a fixture. Do not measure success through forced accounts or pressure to join Regen Commons.

## 5. Revenue and resourcing

The aim remains sustainable public-interest infrastructure and paid stewardship. The following are **funding hypotheses**, not prices or forecasts:

| Possible support | Value exchanged | Evidence needed before relying on it |
|---|---|---|
| Grants for open coordination and interoperability | Open adapters, documented schemas, usable private workflows and public research | A relevant current call, eligible receiving entity, realistic delivery budget and named maintainer. |
| Regen Commons membership support | Members collectively sustain a useful commons service | Council-approved dues and access policy; evidence of voluntary utility, without a compulsory Certified-project gate. |
| Sponsored network cohorts or facilitation | Help teams articulate asks, form partnerships and keep information current | A sponsor who will fund the work; measured steward effort and repeat usefulness. |
| Funder/network service subscriptions | Later curated regional views, call management or consented aggregate reports | Discovery with prospective users and a paid pilot. Never sell private drafts, preferential ranking or project access. |
| Partner-funded integration work | A reusable adapter or jointly tested schema | Written scope, maintenance responsibility and funding. Public APIs do not imply partner engineering capacity. |
| Community donations | Support an open utility | Low-cost administration and transparent reporting; uncertain recurring yield. |

Pool fees and endowment ideas from the old canvas remain outside the first-release operating model. No wallet, token issuance, custody or transaction fee is needed for profile reuse. The protocol is described as open/free to build on; hosting, maintenance and optional integration help still have costs. [Hypercerts contact/FAQ, accessed 6 November](https://hypercerts.org/contact), [Certified welcome, accessed 6 November](https://certified.app/welcome).

Prepare a budget from measured builder hours, steward hours, support cases, storage/API traffic and actual service quotations after the pilot. Define a named maintainer and funded period before expanding external dependencies. A grant that funds only initial construction does not establish sustainability.

## 6. Key resources

Retain the maintained funder catalog and research workflow, existing matching code, Regen Commons design system, website membership infrastructure, relationships and stewardship knowledge. The private save/reload flow is already a resource worth protecting.

New resources include the proposed Certified-only sign-in bridge and its durable state, followed by a small public-read adapter, stable external-reference mapping, dated projections, interoperable ask vocabulary if agreed, and evidence from consenting pilot teams. The external protocol and services are dependencies, not assets owned by Regen Commons. Project records remain attributable to their publishers; data portability does not confer a license to redistribute every image or precise location. [Ownership model](technical-spec.md#3-source-of-truth-and-system-boundaries).

## 7. Key activities

Continue catalog curation, matching-quality review, device testing, steward support and clear privacy practice. For this proposal, add compatibility checks against pinned upstream schemas, safe source refresh, usable correction paths, and measurement of whether reuse actually reduces work.

After Phase 0, test team continuity and concurrency before expanding public exchange. Co-design a typed public ask with a second reader only when there is a real use case. Preserve device drafts and versioned saved documents when adding optional profile reuse. Prioritize the bounded [Certified sign-in/private-saving build](../../05-technical-spec.md#51-first-build-certified-sign-in-and-private-saving), with no account migration or alternate sign-in. Defer speculative synchronization and team work.

## 9. Cost structure

| Cost driver | Pilot treatment | Expansion trigger |
|---|---|---|
| Builder time and critical review | Certified-only authentication, private-account authorization and focused checks; then a bounded read-only adapter | OAuth, team commands and public write paths require additional review and maintenance. |
| Steward/facilitator time | Record time spent helping each participant, including access/correction issues | Scale only when a funded support owner exists. |
| Hosting, data and email | Use current authorized services; measure incremental costs | Reprice when usage or support commitments exceed current capacity. |
| Schema/API change and outage handling | Pin compatibility, maintain fixtures, keep unlinked/offline path | Add service monitoring and agreed support expectations before a dependency becomes essential. |
| Privacy, reuse and governance | Review public-text reuse and future consent wording | Public media, external publication, team disputes and partner agreements add work. |
| Opportunity cost | Protect 20 November evidence work and 30 November Phase 0 | Defer integration if it delays the core release. |

## Pilot and decision rules

Proposed cohort: **eight teams**, six with public project records and two without. Invite only after approval and consent. The first build proves Certified-only sign-in and private Save/Load. The later cohort tests optional read-only profile reuse plus private needs/offers and Save/Load. Two teams may later test collaboration with synthetic data; this is not a requirement to ship teams before 30 November.

Use a lightweight observation sheet with participant codes, task outcomes, durations and support minutes. Do not collect transcripts, private ask content or public identifiers in analytics. Ask separately before recording sessions. Small numbers guide a decision; they do not establish causal effects or market size.

| Metric | Proposed go criterion | What failure teaches |
|---|---|---|
| Correct reuse | At least 5 of 6 linked teams choose the intended project without help; zero incorrect automatic links | Improve identity cues and lookup before expanding. |
| Reduced repeated entry | Median time to a checked profile at least 30% lower than ordinary intake in a counterbalanced comparable task | Profile reuse may not justify its interface cost. Account for learning/order effects. |
| Private saving and recovery | At least 6 of 8 independently save and load on a second device; zero lost edits or cross-account disclosure | Any privacy/data-loss defect stops release; usability failure needs another round. |
| Understanding | At least 7 of 8 correctly explain which details are public and where asks are stored after the task | Revise copy and separation; do not introduce public export yet. |
| Useful repeat use | At least 4 of 8 return within 14 days and make a meaningful update or explicit accuracy confirmation | Reuse alone may not create ongoing value; interview for unmet tasks. |
| Fresher source data | Track stale fields found, correction attempts and changes verified at source; target 2 completed corrections only if at least 2 genuine stale cases exist | Do not manufacture edits to meet a metric. If there are no cases, mark the hypothesis untested. |
| Support burden | Median assisted time under 15 minutes per team for the initial tasks | Simplify the workflow or budget for facilitated delivery. Threshold is a proposal. |
| Team continuity, later phase | Both test teams complete a second-editor update, conflict recovery and editor departure without sharing credentials | Do not open team editing until failure paths are resolved. |
| Interoperability, later phase | One named second application reads the same public ask and its updated/withdrawn status correctly | Keep Weave records private/local until a consumer and common contract exist. |

**Go:** all privacy/recovery gates pass, correct-project selection and save/load targets pass, and there is evidence of reduced entry work. Continue a small read-only pilot while assessing repeat use. **Iterate:** safe but confusing or low-value flows get another small round. **No-go for Phase 0 integration:** any privacy/data-loss defect, unresolved contract compatibility, or a threat to the core deadline. Keep the existing unlinked flow and revisit after launch. **No-go for public writes:** no agreed schema, no second reader, unclear authority, or consent/retraction people do not understand.

Regen Commons owns the go/no-go decision. Suggested owners are the lead builder for technical evidence, a council-named user representative for experience, and the data-policy owner for privacy. Partner teams confirm their own supported contracts and participation. Do not convert these suggested roles into assigned work without agreement.

## Governance and value returned

Keep code and schema work reviewable, attribute source data and return corrections to the appropriate publisher through an agreed route. Share aggregate pilot findings and reusable adapter lessons, not private participant material. The canonical project profile should stay useful across applications; Weave should contribute coordination rather than compete for custody of the same profile.

Council decisions remain in [open decisions](../../open-decisions.md): access tiers, membership, licensing, private-data handling, representation disputes and who funds ongoing support. Partner source policies remain theirs. This draft adds options for review without closing those decisions.
